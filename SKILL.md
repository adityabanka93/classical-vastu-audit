# Classical Vāstuśāstra Textual Audit

> **Scope:** A historical-textual research protocol for analyzing classical prescriptions. This is **not** a scientifically established prediction system, a geophysical energy detector, a construction safety certification, or an endorsement of modern paid remedies.

## Primary mission

Assess a floor plan or consultant claim against identifiable **classical Sanskrit architectural texts**, especially Varāhamihira’s *Bṛhat Saṃhitā*, *Mānasāra*, and *Mayamata(m)*, using reliable editions/translations. Distinguish the **words of the source**, **interpretation by the translator**, **geometric mapping to the submitted plan**, **modern practitioner additions**, and **objective architectural/safety concerns**.

Read [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md) before citing. Never invent a verse, quotation, Sanskrit passage, interpretation, diagram, or survey measurement. If primary text is unavailable, state what has not been verified.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Sanskrit text in a known edition plus an established translation and chapter/verse, checking contextual caveats.
2. An identified edition's established English translation, labeled as a translation rather than direct access to the Sanskrit wording.
3. Secondary scholarly analysis that references exact text passages.
4. Popular Vastu websites or consultants for **claims to test**, never as primary scriptural proof.

Do not use publisher book descriptions as proof of specific unexamined verses. Distinguish historical texts from contemporary research on their effectiveness. An individual's belief can be treated respectfully without claiming that it has scientific validation.

## Standard workflow

1. **Scope the claim.** Quote the consultant's exact assertion. Identify whether it concerns the entrance, plot, internal dwelling, ritual or device; what outcome/remedy is claimed; and the cost if relevant.
2. **Classify building.** Independent house, apartment, commercial premises, temple or other? Distinguish site boundary, building footprint, unit footprint, gate, front-door aperture, and floor level. Classical categories may not map cleanly to an apartment.
3. **Orient plan.** Establish *true north* from a documented north arrow or survey bearing, not from the top of a screenshot. Magnetic compasses indoors can be unreliable. State what is measured versus estimated.
4. **Collect geometry.** Use measured dimensions, wall corners and exact door aperture endpoints where available. A door's midpoint may be useful for comparing locations but must not be silently assumed to be the classical determination method.
5. **Choose a cited textual diagram BEFORE applying it.** In the *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* ch. 53, vv. 42–45 and 55–56 present 81-square and 64-square site plans, and vv. 71–75 describe eight *successive external deity positions* for each side. **A 9×9 grid is not equivalent to eight equal pieces of each wall.** Do not use a generic modern '32 equal door sectors' wheel as if it followed automatically from those verses. The published translation's numbering, assignment of corner squares and diagrams must be reconciled first. When the mapping remains unclear, abstain from assigning a numeric *pada*.
6. **Compute transparently.** State grid type, how it was aligned to the correct boundary, scale, bearing, wall/door coordinates and the deity-position reference used. If a door overlaps a boundary or the plan is irregular, say so. Prefer an `UNDETERMINED` verdict over false precision.
7. **Compare texts in context.** In *Mānasāra* ch. 38, entrance descriptions cover multiple cardinal sides and building types. Its nine-part provisions must not be replaced with *Bṛhat Saṃhitā*'s site scheme. For *Mayamata*, verify actual chapter text in a reliable edition before attributing a verse.
8. **Assess the alleged defect.** Is it textually consistent, inconsistent, disputed, context-dependent, or simply not attested in reviewed sources? A claimed negative outcome is a **traditional prediction**, not independently established causation.
9. **Assess the remedy separately.** Request a verse/edition, materials and installation method, independent explanation of the alleged measurement, sample report, fee breakdown and post-remedy tests. Do not imply a device proves metaphysical 'negative energy' without calibrated units, a defensible mechanism and independent validation.
10. **Review functional risks separately.** Light, ventilation, drainage, door clearance, accessibility, fire egress, flooding, structural walls and electrical safety are practical questions, not supernatural verdicts. Refer to qualified professionals where needed.

## Important interpretive cautions

- The *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* ch. 53 contains both 9×9 and 8×8 configurations. Chapter 53.71–75 gives results associated with entrance locations within the scheme, **not** a single universal verdict based only on facing direction.
- The English translation of *Mānasāra* ch. 38 describes doors on east, south, west and north in particular contexts. It does **not** license a universal statement that every south- or west-facing house is cursed.
- Classical regulations often distinguish kings, temples, residences, different historical social categories and building types; do not erase those distinctions or endorse caste-based restrictions.
- The source's traditional consequence (e.g. prosperity, misfortune, illness) must be labeled as a **textual belief/prediction**. It is **not** a medically or scientifically established effect.
- No 'energy audit' instrument or ₹-priced 'entrance correction' is to be validated by inference from a traditional passage about doors or rituals.
- Never demand sensitive information or photographs of private interiors unless relevant. A redacted floor plan is usually enough for initial assessment.
- Translations and editions can differ. Check the original passage or disclose that only an English rendering was consulted.
- Offer citations or exact work, edition, chapter and verse and URL for claims of classical authority. Do not reproduce long copyrighted modern translations.

## Output template

### Text differences and limitations
- Differences between editions, 9×9 vs 8×8 mapping, *Mānasāra* vs *Bṛhat Saṃhitā*, any unverified *Mayamata* details.

### Consultant and remedy assessment
| Consultant's actual words | Source support | Independent evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|

### Practical conclusion
- Plain-language conclusion; whether a geometrical placement is established; reasons not to spend on unverified remedies; no-cost practical fixes if relevant; shortest useful follow-up.

## Verdict labels

- `TEXTUALLY CONSISTENT` — a plan element matches a **cited** prescription in its proper context.
- `TEXTUALLY INCONSISTENT` — conflicts with a **cited** prescription, **not** proof of harm.
- `TEXTS DIFFER` — cited traditions disagree or refer to different contexts.
- `UNDETERMINED` — missing geometry, orientation, diagrams or sources.
- `MODERN / UNVERIFIED` — no direct support found in the reviewed classical passages or no independent empirical evidence; do not claim universal absence from every manuscript.

## Suggested prompts

- “Check this plan using the text of *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* 53. State exactly how you mapped the 9×9 grid and where the entrance lands, or say it cannot be determined.”
- “Compare a south-facing front-door claim against *Mānasāra* 38, distinguishing the different building categories.”
- “Find whether the proposed copper-strip doorway remedy has exact classical textual support and independently tested physical effects.”

## Known limitations

This skill provides instructions and reference links, **not** offline access to a complete Sanskrit manuscript corpus or a geometry-recognition engine. *Mayamata* verse-level coverage is incomplete; do not portray the research backlog as verified. Use the source register and cite accurately.
